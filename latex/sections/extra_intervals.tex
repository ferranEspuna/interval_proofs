\subsection{The specialized fixed-base experiment}

The script \code{equally_spaced_tiny_interval_lp.py} was written afterward to
test the shape suggested by the general program over a much larger range.  It
fixes the equally spaced base parameters \(q,\delta,a\) from
Section~\ref{sec:equally-spaced-construction}, adds a chosen number of free
intervals, and imposes the lifted constraints from
Section~\ref{sec:milp-formulation}.  The extra intervals are not assigned to
gaps in advance, so binary variables impose their ordering and disjointness.
This is a local extension model: it asks whether this fixed base can be
enlarged, not whether the base is globally optimal.

The zero-gap one-extra scan tested every \(3\leq m\leq100\), using one
representative anchor because the other anchor choices are translates.  On
the larger optimizing choice of \(q\), it found a positive gain precisely for
\(m\equiv0,1\pmod4\), except for \(m=5\).  The observed gain was
\[
 \Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m4}{\frac1{192}\quad(m=4)},\qquad
 \frac{2}{m^2(m+2)}\quad(4\mid m,\ m\geq8),
\]
and
\[
 \frac{1}{m^2(m+2)}
 \quad(m\equiv1\!\!\pmod4,\ m\geq9).
\]
Linked representatives of the latter two families are the
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m8}{m=8}\) and
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m9}{m=9}\) constructions.
The numerical gains agreed with these fractions to within
\(2\times10^{-11}\).  If \(4\mid m\), two adjacent choices of \(q\) maximize
the base density.  The smaller choice \(q=m/4\) admitted no gain above
\(10^{-9}\) in any tested case; the improvement occurs on the larger choice
\(q=m/4+1\).  For example, these are respectively the
\(\Visualizer{equally-spaced-m12-q3}{q=3}\) and
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m12}{q=4}\) branches at
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m12}{m=12}\).
All 123 saved one-extra assignments, including both tied
branches, also pass an independent replay of their bounds, integrality, and
linear constraints.

The complete two-extra scan used the larger branch for every
\(3\leq m\leq100\) and the smaller branch in every tied case, again giving
123 runs.  The first 44 were solved without a specialized upper bound.  For
the remaining 79, each extra length was bounded by the independently computed
one-extra optimum.  This is a valid redundant constraint: deleting the other
extra leaves a feasible one-extra extension of the same base.  Both extras
are positive from \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m12}{m=12}\) onward in the
two relevant congruence classes, and
the total gain is
\[
 \frac{4}{m^2(m+2)}\quad(4\mid m,\ m\geq12),
 \qquad
 \frac{2}{m^2(m+2)}
 \quad(m\equiv1\!\!\pmod4,\ m\geq13).
\]
Linked representatives are the \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m12}{m=12}\)
and \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m13}{m=13}\) constructions.
For \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m4}{m=4}\),
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m8}{m=8}\), and
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m9}{m=9}\), the second variable has zero
length.  No smaller tied branch
improves.  All 123 canonical runs report optimal status and zero stored gap;
their assignments pass independent constraint replay.  Strict no-presolve
reruns replaced noisy canonical results at \(m=34,40,100\), while the original
outputs were retained as diagnostics.  In particular, presolve at \(m=100\)
had stopped one interval exactly \(10^{-6}\) short despite reporting
optimality, illustrating why exact reconstruction and replay are necessary.

The one- and two-extra artifacts, summaries, and replay reports are in
\code{json_results/verification_20260716/tiny_intervals/} and
\code{tiny_intervals_two_extra/}.  Exact fraction arithmetic verifies every
positive two-extra output.  At
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m12}{m=12}\), the two intervals have length
\(1/1008\), giving density
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m12}{73/504}\); at
\(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m13}{m=13}\), both have length
\(1/2535\), giving \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m13}{122/845}\).

\subsection{More extras and exact families}

A third extra interval changes the first of these examples.  For
\(\Visualizer{a12}{m=12}\),
the specialized model finds lengths
\[
 \frac1{1008},\qquad \frac5{12096},\qquad \frac1{1008},
\]
and hence the seven-interval density
\[
 \Visualizer{a12}{\frac17+\frac{29}{12096}=\frac{251}{1728}}.
\]
All 196 lifted interactions of the rationalized union have been checked
exactly.  At \(\Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m13}{m=13}\), a third variable
remains zero.  At \(\Visualizer{m16-three-extra-incumbent}{m=16}\) and
\(\Visualizer{m17-three-extra-incumbent}{m=17}\), three extras are positive,
and subsequent experiments reveal a longer regular pattern.

To state that pattern, let \(B_m\) denote the larger optimizing equally
spaced base and put \(b=a+1/m\), with representatives chosen on the real
line.  If \(4\mid m\), set
\[
 q=\frac m4+1,
 \qquad \delta=\frac1{2(m+2)},
 \qquad g=\frac2{m^2(m+2)},
 \qquad
 s_j=b-\frac{m-4j}{4m^2}.
\]
For every tested \(m=16,20,\ldots,100\), the union of \(B_m\) with the
intervals of length \(g\) starting at
\[
 s_j\quad\hbox{and}\quad s_j+\frac1m,
 \qquad 2\leq j\leq\frac{m-4}{4},
\]
is \(m\)-sum-free.  It has \((m-8)/2\) extra intervals and density
\begin{equation}
 \frac{m^3+4m^2+8m-64}{8m^2(m+2)}
 =\mu(B_m)+\frac{m-8}{m^2(m+2)}.
 \label{eq:mod0-extra-family}
\end{equation}
A direct example of this family is the
\(\Visualizer{mod0-doubled-tail-m16}{m=16}\) construction.

If \(m\equiv1\pmod4\), set
\[
 q=\frac{m+3}{4},
 \qquad \delta=\frac{m+1}{2m(m+2)},
 \qquad g=\frac1{m^2(m+2)},
 \qquad
 s_j=b-\frac{m-(4j+1)}{4m^2}.
\]
For every tested \(m=13,17,\ldots,97\), take the intervals of length \(g\)
starting at
\[
 s_j\quad\hbox{and}\quad s_j+\frac1m,
 \qquad 2\leq j\leq\frac{m-5}{4}.
\]
Together with \(B_m\), these form an exactly checked \(m\)-sum-free union
with \((m-9)/2\) extras and density
\begin{equation}
 \frac{m^3+4m^2+7m-36}{8m^2(m+2)}
 =\mu(B_m)+\frac{m-9}{2m^2(m+2)}.
 \label{eq:mod1-extra-family}
\end{equation}
A direct example of this family is the
\(\Visualizer{mod1-doubled-tail-m13}{m=13}\) construction.
The endpoint and lift verifiers checked more than \(2.28\) million images for
these two finite families using exact rational arithmetic.  Their reports are
archived in \code{tiny_intervals_mod0_doubled_tail_exact/} and
\code{tiny_intervals_mod1_full_family_exact/}.  These checks prove the listed
lower-bound constructions; they do not prove the formulas for untested
multipliers or establish local optimality.

The aggregate gains in~\eqref{eq:mod0-extra-family}
and~\eqref{eq:mod1-extra-family} are \(O(m^{-2})\), even though the number of
extra components is linear in \(m\).  They therefore do not change the
\(1/8\) limit of the equally spaced densities.  This does not show that every
possible improvement is \(O(m^{-2})\); merely proving that the best aggregate
improvement is \(o(1)\) would suffice to preserve the limit \(1/8\).

As a numerical saturation check, the fixed-base model was solved with one
more extra variable than the number eventually used in the following cases:

\subsection{Unrestricted seven-interval audit at
\texorpdfstring{$m=12$}{m=12}}

The fixed-base statement above does not establish optimality among all unions
of seven intervals.  To test that distinction directly, let \(M_N(12)\) be the
numerical optimum of the unrestricted standard model with at most \(N\)
components.  The archived smaller models give the following rational
reconstructions:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}c c c@{}}
\toprule
\(N\) & computed \(M_N(12)\) & source of the bound \\
\midrule
2 & \Visualizer{equally-spaced-m12-q2}{\(5/42\)}
  & direct unrestricted run \\
3 & \Visualizer{equally-spaced-m12-q3}{\(1/7\)}
  & squeezed by the construction and the \(N=4\) run \\
4 & \Visualizer{equally-spaced-m12-q4}{\(1/7\)}
  & direct unrestricted run \\
5 & \Visualizer{fixed-base-1-extra-m12}{\(145/1008\)}
  & direct unrestricted run \\
6 & \Visualizer{fixed-base-2-extra-m12}{\(73/504\)}
  & direct unrestricted run \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Each direct run reports optimal status and zero floating-point MIP gap; these
remain numerical bounds, not exact branch certificates.  The \(N=7\) audit
added the corresponding bound to every subunion of sizes \(2,\ldots,6\), and
used the exact construction \(\Visualizer{a12}{251/1728}\) as a total-length
cutoff.  These are
valid accelerations conditional on the smaller numerical bounds: every
subunion of an \(m\)-sum-free set is again \(m\)-sum-free.

The first monolithic strengthened search exhausted memory because its open
branch-and-bound tree continued to grow.  The completed audit instead used the
translated-missing-point formulation, chose positional index \(0\) to be a
globally shortest interval, and split on the indices of a second- and then a
third-shortest interval.  There are \(6\cdot5=30\) ordered prefixes.  Every
length pattern occurs in at least one prefix; ties only make the cases overlap.
The reflection convention \(\alpha_1\leq\alpha_6\) removes a further symmetry.
All endpoints and lengths otherwise remain free.

Each case used the same smaller-subunion bounds and the exact construction
cutoff \(\Visualizer{a12}{\sum_i\alpha_i\geq251/1728}\).  All 30 cases closed:
26 are infeasible at or above the cutoff, while the prefixes
\((1,3),(1,5),(3,1),(5,1)\) are zero-gap optimal at
\(\Visualizer{a12}{251/1728}\).
The word ``infeasible'' here means that the ordering class contains no point
at or above the cutoff, not that it contains no \(m\)-sum-free union.  The four
optimal prefixes are overlapping symmetry and tie representations of the same
length multiset,
\[
 \Visualizer{a12}{
 4\times\frac1{28},\qquad
 2\times\frac1{1008},\qquad
 \frac5{12096}}.
\]
The final attempts processed \(42749286\) nodes in \(121154.13\) aggregate
solver-seconds, and none ended at a time or node limit.  The 30 retained
problem, solution, and log triples are in
\code{json_results/long_runs/m12_N7_length_order_depth2/}.

Consequently, conditional on the smaller-\(N\) numerical upper bounds, the
all-endpoints-free model has the zero-gap numerical optimum
\[
 \Visualizer{a12}{M_7(12)=\frac{251}{1728}}.
\]
This is an optimum among unions of at most seven intervals, not merely among
extensions of the fixed equally spaced base.  Because the smaller-subunion
cuts come from rational reconstructions of floating-point solves, however,
the upper bound is not yet an unconditional exact certificate for the literal
model.  The exact rational construction and hence the lower bound
\(\Visualizer{a12}{M_7(12)\geq251/1728}\) are independent of that
qualification.

\subsection{Why the exceptional short length occurs at
\texorpdfstring{$m=12$}{m=12}}

The \Visualizer{a12}{exact endpoint certificate} explains the fraction
\(\Visualizer{a12}{5/12096}\).  Write
\(\delta=1/28\), \(g=1/1008\), let \(B\) be the base interval starting at
\(x=29/140\), let \(V\) be the full extra starting at \(v=139/840\), and let
the short extra \(T\) start at \(y=7103/60480\) with length \(h\).  Two tight
lifted endpoint inequalities in the certificate are
\[
 2x-12(y+h)=-1,
 \qquad
 (v+g)+(x+\delta)-12y=-1.
\]
Subtracting them gives
\[
 12h=x-(v+g)-\delta
     =\frac1{24}-g-\delta
     =\frac5{1008},
 \qquad \Visualizer{a12}{h=\frac5{12096}}.
\]
Thus the short component is the residual of a half-cell gap after one base
width and one full-extra width have been consumed, divided by the multiplier
12.  It is pinned by active certificate equations, not merely recognized by
fitting a fraction to solver output.

There is also a structural explanation for why this length appears only in
the observed optimum at \(\Visualizer{a12}{m=12}\).  Put \(m=4k\), and for
the larger optimized
base set
\[
 \delta=\frac1{2(m+2)},
 \qquad g=\frac2{m^2(m+2)}.
\]
A natural hybrid branch (for example,
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m12}{m=12}\)) has \(k-1\) full extras of length
\(g\) and one short
bridge of length
\[
 h=\frac{m-2}{m^3(m+2)}
   =\left(\frac12-\frac1m\right)g.
\]
Its gain over the base is
\[
 G_H=\frac{m^2-2m-4}{2m^3(m+2)}.
\]
The regular doubled-tail branch (for example,
\(\Visualizer{mod0-doubled-tail-m16}{m=16}\)) instead has \(2(k-2)\) full
extras and gain
\[
 G_D=\frac{m-8}{m^2(m+2)}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
 G_H-G_D
 =\frac{-m^2+14m-4}{2m^3(m+2)}.
\]
It is positive at \(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m12}{m=12}\), where it equals
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m12}{5/12096}\), but is already
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m16}{-1/4096}\) at
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m16}{m=16}\) and stays negative thereafter.  Exact
fraction replay checks the hybrid for all 23 values
\(m=12,16,\ldots,100\) (linked examples:
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m12}{m=12}\) and
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m16}{m=16}\)), covering 468257
lifted images.  In particular, a short bridge remains feasible beyond
\(\Visualizer{mod0-hybrid-m12}{m=12}\); it disappears from the optimal lists
because the \Visualizer{mod0-doubled-tail-m16}{all-full regular tail} has
become larger.  This explains the observed branch switch, but does
not prove that these two branches exhaust all fixed-base or unrestricted
constructions.

The remaining theoretical questions are to prove the displayed families
uniformly, establish exhaustive fixed-base upper bounds, and decide whether
free endpoints or entirely different constructions give a larger aggregate
improvement.  In particular, it remains open whether \(d_m(\T)\to1/8\).
